Duck Epics — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-04

Duck Epics ("the Power-Up") is a Trello Power-Up that adds a Subscription → Sub-task hierarchy to Trello boards. This policy explains what data it uses and how it is stored.

What data we process

  • Board relationship data. The Power-Up stores parent/child links between cards, a Subscription icon, per-sub-task links, and UI preferences. This is saved using Trello's own pluginData storage, attached to your board and to your Trello member profile — it never leaves Trello's infrastructure.
  • Trello authorization token. To create cards, move cards, set due dates, assign members and read archived cards, the Power-Up asks you to authorize it. The resulting token is stored member-private inside Trello and is used only to call the Trello REST API on your behalf. It is never sent to us or any third party.
  • Card content shown in the UI. Card names, lists, due dates, checklists, labels and member avatars are read from Trello to display the Power-Up interface. They are processed in your browser and are not stored by us.

Where data is stored

The Power-Up has no server or database of its own. All data lives inside Trello (board/member pluginData). The Power-Up's code is static files served over HTTPS from GitHub Pages; those requests deliver code to your browser and do not transmit your Trello data to us.

What we do NOT do

  • We do not run analytics or tracking.
  • We do not set cookies.
  • We do not sell, share or transmit your data to third parties.
  • We do not store your Trello token or card content on our side.

Data retention & deletion

Because data is stored in Trello's pluginData, removing the Power-Up from a board, or clearing its authorization in your Trello account settings, removes its access. Relationship data can be cleared with the "Unmark" action or by removing the Power-Up.

Third-party services

The Power-Up uses the Trello REST API (Atlassian) and is hosted on GitHub Pages. Your use of Trello is also governed by Atlassian's privacy policy.
